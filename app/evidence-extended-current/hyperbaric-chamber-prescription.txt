L12: # Do You Need a Prescription for a Hyperbaric Chamber?
L13: 
L14: Yes. Hyperbaric chambers are FDA-regulated Class II medical devices, and OxyHealth chambers are sold on the order of a licensed healthcare provider, for home use and clinic use alike. The prescription is part of how the device is regulated. It is not a formality.
L15: ## Why a prescription is required
L16: 
L17: A hyperbaric chamber raises the pressure around the whole body above normal atmospheric pressure. That makes it a medical device. FDA lists hyperbaric chambers under Product Code CBF and regulation 【7†21 CFR 868.5470†www.accessdata.fda.gov】 as Class II devices that go through 510(k) premarket review.
L18: FDA 510(k) clearance concerns the device itself: its design, performance, labeling, and manufacturing controls. The prescription requirement sits alongside that clearance. It makes sure a licensed healthcare provider is involved before a person begins using a pressurized device.
L19: ## Calling a chamber a wellness product does not change this
L20: 
L21: Some sellers describe full-body chambers as wellness or lifestyle products. A marketing label does not change how FDA classifies a hyperbaric chamber. If you are comparing sellers, ask for the device's 510(k) clearance and confirm it in 【7†FDA's device classification database†www.accessdata.fda.gov】, and ask how the seller handles the prescription requirement.
L22: ## What the prescription covers, and what it does not
L23: 
L24: A prescription authorizes a person to obtain and use the device under a clinician's direction. The clinician, not the manufacturer, determines:
L25: 
L26:   * Whether hyperbaric therapy is appropriate for the individual
L27:   * The breathing gas and any supplemental oxygen
L28:   * The pressure, stated in ATA
L29:   * Session length, frequency, and total number of sessions
L30: These variables together make up the dose. Pressure and oxygen concentration are separate variables, and two protocols that share a name can deliver very different exposures. OxyHealth does not prescribe protocols or recommend session counts.
L31: ## Device clearance and clinical use are different things
L32: 
L33: FDA clearance applies to a device and its cleared labeling. How a physician uses a cleared device for a particular patient is a separate medical judgment. Research on conditions outside a device's cleared indications does not mean the device is cleared for those uses, and a physician's decision to use a cleared device does not make the device uncleared.

L41: ## Hyperbaric Chamber Prescription FAQ
L42: 
L43: ### Do you need a prescription to buy a hyperbaric chamber?
L44: 
L45: Yes, for OxyHealth chambers. Hyperbaric chambers are FDA-regulated Class II medical devices, and OxyHealth chambers are sold on the order of a licensed healthcare provider. This applies to home use as well as clinic use.
L46: ### Do I need a prescription for a home hyperbaric chamber?
L47: 
L48: Yes. A chamber does not stop being a medical device because it is used at home or described as a wellness product. OxyHealth requires a prescription from a licensed healthcare provider for its home chambers.
L49: ### Does the prescription decide my pressure and session schedule?
L50: 
L51: Your clinician does. A qualified clinician determines whether hyperbaric therapy is appropriate for you and sets the breathing gas, pressure, session length, and frequency. OxyHealth does not prescribe protocols.
L52: ### Is a prescription the same as insurance coverage?
L53: 
L54: No. A prescription is a requirement for obtaining the device. Insurance coverage is a separate decision made by the payer and depends on the condition, the setting, and the plan.
