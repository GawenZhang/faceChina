L14: The Science
L15: ## How Home Hyperbaric Chambers Work
L16: 
L17: Home hyperbaric chambers deliver mild hyperbaric oxygen therapy (mHBOT) at 1.3 atmospheres absolute (ATA). Based on Henry's Law of physics, this elevated pressure causes significantly more oxygen to dissolve into blood plasma than is possible under normal atmospheric conditions.
L18: Setting up a home hyperbaric chamber is straightforward. OxyHealth portable chambers inflate using a compact, purpose-built compressor that plugs into a standard household electrical outlet. No special wiring, dedicated circuits, or professional installation is required. An oxygen concentrator connects to the chamber to deliver concentrated oxygen during the session.
L19: Once inflated, you simply enter the chamber, seal the zipper closure, and relax while the compressor maintains the therapeutic pressure of 1.3 ATA throughout the session.
L20: A typical session lasts between 60 and 90 minutes. During this time, many users read, watch content on a tablet or phone, listen to music or podcasts, meditate, or simply rest. The chamber features transparent viewing panels that allow natural light in and reduce any feeling of confinement. After the session, the chamber can remain inflated for the next use or be deflated and stored compactly when not needed. The entire system is designed to integrate seamlessly into your daily life at home.

L65: Getting Started
L66: ## Setting Up Your Home Chamber
L67: 
L68: One of the biggest advantages of OxyHealth portable chambers is how easy they are to set up and use at home. No construction, no permits, and no special infrastructure is needed. Here is what to consider when preparing your space for a home hyperbaric chamber.
L69: ### Room Requirements
L70: 
L71: Choose a clean, dry, well-ventilated room with enough floor space to accommodate the inflated chamber plus adequate clearance for easy entry and exit. A spare bedroom, home office, or dedicated wellness room works well. The Vitaeris 320 needs approximately 8 feet of length and 4 feet of width. The compact Respiro 270 fits in even smaller spaces. Maintain a comfortable room temperature, as the chamber interior can warm slightly during sessions.
L72: ### Electrical Needs
L73: 
L74: OxyHealth home chambers operate on standard household electrical outlets (110-120V). You will need access to two outlets near your chamber location: one for the compressor that pressurizes the chamber and one for the oxygen concentrator. No special wiring, dedicated circuits, or electrician visits are required. The equipment runs quietly and draws a modest amount of power, comparable to common household appliances.
L75: ### Daily Routine
L76: 
L77: Most home users establish a consistent daily routine. Inflate the chamber using the compressor, enter the chamber, seal the zipper closure, and allow the pressure to build to 1.3 ATA. Relax for 60 to 90 minutes during your session. Afterward, the chamber can remain inflated for the next use or be deflated and stored. Many users keep their chamber inflated throughout the week for maximum convenience, deflating it when the space is needed for other purposes.

L120: A typical home HBOT session lasts between 60 and 90 minutes. During the session, users relax inside the chamber while breathing concentrated oxygen at 1.3 ATA. Many people read, watch content on a tablet, listen to music or podcasts, meditate, or simply rest. The chamber features transparent viewing panels that allow natural light in and help reduce any feeling of confinement.
L121: The frequency and total number of sessions should be determined in consultation with a healthcare provider based on individual needs and goals.
L122: ### What is the difference between a home chamber and a clinical chamber?
L123: Home hyperbaric chambers, also called portable chambers, operate at mild pressure levels of 1.3 ATA and are designed for safe, daily residential use. Clinical chambers, used in hospitals, wound care centers, and medical facilities, can reach pressures up to 3.0 ATA and are typically operated by trained technicians. Both types increase oxygen delivery to body tissues based on the same underlying physics (Henry's Law), but clinical chambers deliver higher pressures for more intensive medical protocols.
L124: The appropriate type depends on the condition being addressed and should be discussed with a healthcare provider.
