L21: ### Can you provide the FDA 510(k) letter for this chamber?
L22: 
L23: Why this matters: This gives buyers confidence that the device has been evaluated for safe clinical use and meets engineering and safety standards under U.S. law. For clinicians, this is necessary for applying for facility accreditation or getting malpractice insurance coverage.
L24: FDA-registered does not mean FDA-cleared. Registration only means the company told the FDA it exists. It does not mean the device is cleared or evaluated to be legally offered within the USA.

L26: Hard Shell Chamber Standards (ASME PVHO-1)
L27: ### Is the chamber ASME PVHO-1 stamped and serialized individually? Do you have the documents to support? (GR-1 Form)
L28: 
L29: Why this matters: This validates the structural integrity, design safety, and compliance of a pressure vessel for human occupancy under pressure. Without this, the chamber's safety under pressure cannot be guaranteed.
L30: This stamp certifies that the hyperbaric chamber is designed using recognized engineering principles, is manufactured by an ASME-accredited facility, and has passed required material, weld and pressure testing from a third-party engineer.
L31: If the company cannot produce a GR-1 form and does not have a PVHO-1 stamp: the vessel was not built to any recognized standard, safety testing was not done or not verified by an independent inspector, and the chamber may be illegal for clinical or commercial use in the United States.

L51: ## Pressure and oxygen concentration are separate variables
L52: 
L53: Hyperbaric therapy starts with a physical fact: the person is inside an environment pressurized above normal atmospheric pressure. Raising the pressure raises the partial pressure of every gas being breathed, including oxygen, even when the breathing gas is ordinary air.
L54: Oxygen concentration is a second, independent variable. Increased pressure raises the partial pressure of inspired oxygen, and a higher inspired oxygen concentration raises it further. Knowing a chamber's pressure does not tell you what gas was breathed, so a statement such as "1.3 ATA" describes pressure only.
L55: The full exposure, or dose, also depends on time at pressure, how often sessions happen, and the total number of sessions. Two studies can both use the word HBOT while delivering very different doses. The 【2†research library】 lists each study's pressure, breathing gas, session length, and schedule for that reason.
L56: ## How the conversions work
L57: 
L58:   * psi (absolute) = ATA x 14.696
L59:   * psi (gauge) = (ATA - 1) x 14.696
L60:   * kPa = ATA x 101.325
L61:   * mmHg = ATA x 760
L62:   * Sea water depth in feet = (ATA - 1) x 33
L63:   * Inspired oxygen partial pressure = ATA x oxygen fraction
L64: 
L65: Chamber gauges show gauge pressure, the amount added above the surrounding atmosphere. Research papers usually state absolute pressure in ATA. Converting between the two is the most common source of confusion when reading chamber specifications.
L66: ## Hyperbaric Pressure FAQ
L67: 
L68: ### How many psi is 1.3 ATA?
L69: 
L70: 1.3 ATA is about 19.1 psi absolute, which is about 4.4 psi above normal atmospheric pressure (gauge pressure). Chamber gauges read gauge pressure, so a chamber at 1.3 ATA shows roughly 4 psi.
L71: 
L72: ### What does ATA mean?
L73: 
L74: ATA stands for atmospheres absolute. 1 ATA is normal atmospheric pressure at sea level. 2 ATA is twice that pressure. ATA includes the atmosphere you are already under, which is why it is called absolute.
L75: ### Is pressure the same as oxygen concentration?
L76: 
L77: No. Pressure and oxygen concentration are separate variables. Pressure is how much the surrounding environment is compressed. Oxygen concentration is the fraction of the breathing gas that is oxygen. Both affect the partial pressure of inspired oxygen.
L78: ### What is the difference between absolute and gauge pressure?
L79: 
L80: Absolute pressure counts the atmosphere itself, so sea level is 1 ATA or 14.7 psi absolute. Gauge pressure counts only the pressure added above the atmosphere, so sea level is 0 psi gauge.

L11: ## How to read a hyperbaric study
L12: 
L13: Hyperbaric therapy means breathing inside an environment pressurized above normal atmospheric pressure. L14:   * Pressure, in ATA
L15:   * Breathing gas: room air, enriched oxygen, or near 100% oxygen
L16:   * Minutes at pressure per session
L17:   * Schedule: sessions per week and total sessions
L18:   * Comparator: sham exposure, no treatment, or none

L18: Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) may support athletic recovery by delivering concentrated oxygen under pressure directly into blood plasma, enabling it to reach injured muscles and tissues that red blood cells cannot easily access. Research suggests this increased oxygen supply may help reduce inflammation and support the repair of sprains, strains, and fractures.
L19: A study published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine (Staples et al., 1999) found HBOT may help accelerate recovery from certain sports injuries.
L20: When an athlete sustains an injury, the surrounding tissue often swells and restricts blood flow, starving the damaged area of the oxygen it needs to heal. HBOT counteracts this by flooding the body with oxygen at pressures up to 1.5 ATA, which dissolves oxygen directly into plasma independent of hemoglobin. This allows oxygen molecules to penetrate swollen, inflamed tissue and reach areas of restricted circulation, jumpstarting the cellular repair process.
L21: At the cellular level, research suggests HBOT may promote the release of growth factors and stem cells, support collagen production for tissue repair, and enhance white blood cell function. The therapy may also help the body clear metabolic waste products more efficiently, potentially reducing post-exercise soreness and supporting athletes in maintaining higher training volumes throughout the season.
