L28: 
L29: Not all hyperbaric therapy providers are equal. Whether you are visiting a clinic near you or evaluating a facility for the first time, asking the right questions can help ensure you receive safe, effective treatment in an FDA-cleared chamber.
L30: ### Ask About Chamber Certifications
L31: 
L32: Verify that the facility uses FDA-cleared hyperbaric chambers. All OxyHealth chambers are FDA 510(k) cleared as Class II medical devices and built to ASME PVHO-1 pressure vessel safety standards. Chambers that are not FDA-cleared may not meet the safety requirements necessary for human use.
L33: ### Check Provider Credentials
L34: 
L35: Ask whether a licensed physician oversees the HBOT protocols at the facility. For medical applications, a doctor experienced in hyperbaric medicine should be involved in developing your treatment plan. Trained technicians should be present during each session to monitor safety.
L36: ### Ask About Treatment Protocols
L37: 
L38: A reputable provider will explain their treatment protocols clearly, including the pressure level used, session duration, number of recommended sessions, and expected outcomes. Protocols should be based on established clinical guidelines and tailored to your individual needs.

L122: ### How do I find hyperbaric therapy near me?
L123: You can find hyperbaric therapy providers by searching for HBOT clinics, wound care centers, or hyperbaric medicine facilities in your area. Hospitals with wound care departments often offer HBOT services. Wellness centers and integrative health practices are also increasingly adding hyperbaric chambers to their offerings. L124: ### What should I look for when choosing an HBOT clinic?
L125: When evaluating an HBOT clinic, ask about the manufacturer and model of their hyperbaric chambers and verify that they use FDA-cleared devices. Check the credentials of the staff administering treatments, including whether a physician oversees the protocols. Ask about the specific treatment protocols they use and how many sessions they recommend. Verify that the facility follows proper safety procedures, including fire safety protocols and emergency procedures.
L126: A reputable clinic will be transparent about all of these details.
L127: ### Is it better to go to a clinic or own a hyperbaric chamber at home?
L128: Both options have advantages. Clinics offer clinical-grade chambers at higher pressures and on-site medical supervision, making them appropriate for FDA-cleared medical conditions. Home ownership offers convenience, consistency, privacy, and eliminates the need to travel for each session. Many people who start at a clinic eventually choose to purchase a home chamber for ongoing wellness use.
L129: OxyHealth manufactures chambers for both clinical and home settings, so the best choice depends on your specific needs and goals.
L130: ### How much does a hyperbaric therapy session cost at a clinic?
L131: Session costs vary widely depending on the provider, location, type of chamber, and whether the treatment is covered by insurance. Insurance typically covers HBOT for FDA-cleared indications such as diabetic foot ulcers, radiation tissue damage, and decompression sickness. For wellness or off-label uses, sessions are generally paid out of pocket. Many people find that the cumulative cost of regular clinic visits makes home chamber ownership a more practical long-term option.
L132: Contact OxyHealth for information about financing options for chamber ownership.
L133: ### Do I need a prescription for hyperbaric oxygen therapy?
L134: For clinical HBOT at higher pressures (above 1.3 ATA), a prescription from a licensed healthcare provider is typically required. Mild hyperbaric therapy at 1.3 ATA, which is available in portable chambers designed for home use, does not require a prescription in most jurisdictions. However, it is always recommended to consult with a healthcare provider before starting any hyperbaric oxygen therapy protocol to ensure it is appropriate for your individual health needs.
L135: ### What types of facilities offer hyperbaric therapy?
L136: Hyperbaric therapy is available at several types of facilities. Hospital-based wound care centers typically offer clinical HBOT for FDA-cleared conditions. Freestanding hyperbaric clinics specialize in HBOT and often treat both medical and wellness clients. Integrative medicine practices and wellness centers increasingly offer mild hyperbaric therapy as part of their services. Chiropractic offices and sports medicine clinics may also have hyperbaric chambers.
L137: Additionally, many individuals choose to use OxyHealth portable chambers at home.
L138: ### How many HBOT sessions will I need?
L139: The number of sessions depends on the reason for treatment and the protocol recommended by a healthcare provider. Medical conditions such as chronic wounds may require 30 to 40 sessions or more. Wellness and recovery protocols commonly involve 20 to 40 sessions. Some people incorporate HBOT into their ongoing routine with regular sessions over an extended period. A healthcare provider can help determine the right protocol for your needs.
